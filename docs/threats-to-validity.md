# Threats to Validity

## Construct Validity

- Evidence confidence and independence are declared inputs; the engine cannot
  prove that an issuer is authoritative or that evidence content is true.
- The assurance score compresses heterogeneous governance claims into one value.
  Control-level results and the graph must therefore remain primary.
- Cross-framework mappings are analyst hypotheses, not equivalence proofs.

## Internal Validity

- The deterministic case isolates engine behaviour but excludes organisational
  processes that would influence a real decision.
- Single and pairwise shocks do not cover all higher-order dependencies.
- The policy-sensitivity grid varies three parameters while holding rebuttal
  penalties and incident costs fixed.

## External Validity

- The synthetic consumer-credit case cannot establish generality for medical,
  employment, public-sector, or foundation-model systems.
- EU AI Act applicability, actor roles, dates, and obligations require current
  jurisdiction-specific legal analysis.
- NIST AI RMF is voluntary and context-sensitive; ISO/IEC 42001 conformity cannot
  be inferred from this public mapping.

## Conclusion Validity

- The sensitivity grid is an engineered robustness study, not a probabilistic
  sample; frequencies across cells are not confidence levels.
- Thresholds are disclosed portfolio assumptions and have no empirical claim to
  optimality.
- A content hash detects change, not false evidence created consistently from the
  outset. External signatures, access controls, and trusted timestamps would be
  required for production provenance.

## Mitigations in This Repository

- conclusions are reported at control and scenario level, not only as one score;
- negative and conditional findings are preserved;
- inputs, formulas, thresholds, shocks, and generated outputs are versioned;
- all tests are deterministic and CI regenerates the evidence pack;
- legal and certification limits are repeated in the README and reports.
